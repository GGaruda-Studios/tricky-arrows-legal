Tricky Arrows

Privacy Policy

Google Play package pastry.trickyarrows.tap.puzzle
Published by Golden Garuda Studios

Last updated 6 October 2026.

The short version

Tricky Arrows keeps your progress on your device. It shows no ads and sells nothing about you. Two things leave your device: anonymous usage and crash data, sent to Google's Firebase, which you can switch off in Settings; and, if you use Google Play Games, a copy of your progress plus your achievements and leaderboard scores, kept in your Google account.

What the app stores on your device

One file, holding only what the game needs to work:

  • which puzzles you have completed, the puzzle you are part-way through, and your best time, mistakes and hints for each completed puzzle
  • your daily-challenge streak and the days you completed it
  • your settings: sound, music, vibration, zen mode, theme, skins, the daily reminder, and whether usage and crash data are shared
  • whether you have seen the tutorial

That file is private to the app. Android removes it when you uninstall Tricky Arrows. Deleting the app's data from Android Settings erases it immediately.

Usage and crash data (you can switch it off)

Tricky Arrows shares anonymous usage and crash data unless you switch it off in Settings → Share usage & crash data. Switching it off stops all further collection straight away.

While it is on, the app uses two Google Firebase services:

  • Firebase Analytics receives game events — a puzzle started, won, failed or left, a life lost, a hint used — with the puzzle number and difficulty. Firebase adds standard technical details: the app version, your device model and Android version, your approximate country (derived from your network address, which is not stored), and a random identifier for this installation of the app. We use this only to see where puzzles are too hard or too easy and to improve the game.
  • Firebase Crashlytics receives a report if the app crashes: what the app was doing, the app version, your device model and Android version, and a random identifier for this installation. We use this only to fix bugs.

Neither contains your name, email address, contacts, photos or location. The app tells Firebase that consent for advertising storage, advertising user data and ad personalization is denied, so nothing collected is used for advertising. Firebase can read Android's advertising identifier on devices where it is available; with advertising consent denied it is not used for ads, and you can delete or reset it in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads.

This data is processed by Google under the Firebase privacy terms and kept no longer than our Firebase project's retention setting allows (at most 14 months). Turning the switch off stops all further collection. To have data already collected from your device deleted, email us at the address below.

Google Play Games (optional)

If you are signed in to Google Play Games on your device, Tricky Arrows uses it to:

  • save your progress online — a copy of the save file described above, stored in your Google account's Play Games saved games, so you can carry on after reinstalling or on a new phone;
  • record achievements such as levels and stars earned; and
  • post scores to leaderboards — levels cleared and stars earned. Other players see your Play Games name and avatar next to your score, as with any Play Games leaderboard.

We do not receive your Google account details. This data is held by Google under Google's Privacy Policy, and you can manage or delete it in the Google Play Games app and in your Google account settings. If you do not use Play Games, nothing is sent.

Daily reminder

Once you have completed a Daily Challenge, the app may ask Android for permission to show notifications. If you allow it, Tricky Arrows schedules one reminder in the evening when today's puzzle is still waiting. The reminder is scheduled on your device by the app itself; no server is involved and nothing is sent. Turn it off in Settings → Daily reminder or in Android's notification settings.

Permissions

  • INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE — for the optional usage and crash data and for Google Play Games.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and WAKE_LOCK — for the daily reminder, including keeping it scheduled after your phone restarts.
  • VIBRATE — for the small haptic taps when you remove an arrow.
  • AD_ID — added by Firebase; see above. The app shows no ads.

If you email us

The Settings screen has a Send feedback link. Tapping it opens your own email app with a blank message addressed to us, and adds three lines at the bottom: the app version, your device model, and your Android version. Nothing is sent unless you write the message and press send yourself, and you can delete those lines first if you prefer.

If you do send us a message, we will see your email address and whatever you wrote, and we use it only to answer you.

Backups

Tricky Arrows leaves Android's standard Auto Backup switched on, so that reinstalling the app or moving to a new phone does not cost you your progress. When Auto Backup is enabled on your device, Android copies the save file described above into your own Google Drive account, in a private area that counts against neither your Drive storage nor our access — we cannot read it. That copy is handled by Google under Google's Privacy Policy, and you can turn Auto Backup off at any time in Android Settings under Google → Backup.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent any information about you.
  • We do not show advertising, and nothing collected is used for advertising.
  • We do not collect your name, email address, contacts, photos or precise location through the app.

Children

Tricky Arrows is a puzzle game for a general audience. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Usage and crash data contain no names or contact details, and can be switched off in Settings. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, email us and we will delete it.

Changes

If a future version of Tricky Arrows changes what it collects or sends, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the app's release notes on Google Play.
